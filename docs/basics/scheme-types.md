# Types of Mutual Fund Schemes

## Mutual Fund Scheme Classification

Mutual funds come in many varieties, designed to meet different investor goals. They can be broadly classified based on:

- **Organisation structure** — Open-ended, Close-ended, Interval
- **Management of portfolio** — Active or Passive
- **Investment objective** — Growth, Income, Liquidity
- **Underlying portfolio** — Equity, Debt, Hybrid, Money market instruments, Multi-Asset
- **Thematic / solution oriented** — Tax saving, Retirement benefit, Child welfare, Arbitrage
- Exchange Traded Funds
- Overseas funds
- Fund of funds

## Classification by Organisation Structure

- **Open-ended schemes** are perpetual, and open for subscription and repurchase on a continuous basis on all business days at the current NAV.
- **Close-ended schemes** have a fixed maturity date. Units are issued at the time of the initial offer and redeemed only on maturity. Units of close-ended schemes are mandatorily listed to provide an exit route before maturity, and can be sold/traded on stock exchanges.
- **Interval schemes** allow purchase and redemption during specified transaction periods (intervals). The transaction period must be a minimum of 2 days, with at least a 15-day gap between two transaction periods. Units of interval schemes are also mandatorily listed on stock exchanges.

## Classification by Portfolio Management

### Active Funds

In an active fund, the fund manager is "active" in deciding whether to buy, hold, or sell the underlying securities and in stock selection. Active funds adopt different strategies and styles to create and manage the portfolio.

- The investment strategy and style are described upfront in the Scheme Information Document (offer document).
- Active funds expect to generate better returns (alpha) than the benchmark index.
- The risk and return of the fund depend on the strategy adopted.
- Active funds implement strategies to "select" stocks for the portfolio.

### Passive Funds

Passive funds hold a portfolio that replicates a stated index or benchmark, e.g.:

- Index Funds
- Exchange Traded Funds (ETFs)

In a passive fund, the fund manager has a passive role, as the stock selection / buy-hold-sell decision is driven by the benchmark index, and the fund manager/dealer merely needs to replicate it with minimal tracking error.

### Active vs. Passive Funds

|            | Active Funds                                                                      | Passive Funds                                                                            |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Management | Rely on professional fund managers who manage investments                         | Investment holdings mirror and closely track a benchmark index (e.g., Index Funds, ETFs) |
| Goal       | Aim to outperform the benchmark index                                             | Aim to allocate exactly as per the market index                                          |
| Suited for | Investors who wish to take advantage of fund managers' alpha generation potential | Investors who want lower expense ratios, lower costs, and better liquidity               |

## Classification by Investment Objectives

Mutual funds offer products that cater to different investment objectives, such as:

- Capital Appreciation (Growth)
- Capital Preservation
- Regular Income
- Liquidity
- Tax-Saving

Mutual funds also offer investment plans, such as Growth and Dividend options, to help tailor the investment to investors' needs.

### Growth Funds

- Designed to provide capital appreciation.
- Primarily invest in growth-oriented assets, such as equity.
- Require a medium-to-long-term investment horizon.
- Historically, equity as an asset class has outperformed most other kinds of investments held over the long term. However, returns from growth funds tend to be volatile in the short term, since the prices of the underlying equity shares may change — investors must be able to tolerate short-term volatility.

### Income Funds

- Objective is to provide regular and steady income to investors.
- Invest in fixed income securities such as corporate bonds, debentures, and government securities.
- Returns come from interest income earned on these investments as well as capital gains from any change in the value of the securities.
- The fund distributes income provided the portfolio generates the required returns — there is no guarantee of income.
- Returns depend on the tenor and credit quality of the securities held.

### Liquid / Overnight / Money Market Mutual Funds

Liquid schemes, overnight funds, and money market mutual funds are investment options for investors seeking liquidity and principal protection, with commensurate returns.

- These funds invest in money market instruments* with maturities not exceeding 91 days.
- Returns depend on the short-term interest rate prevalent in the market.
- Ideal for investors who wish to park surplus funds for short periods. Investors who use these funds for longer holding periods may be sacrificing better returns possible from products suited to a longer holding period.

\* Money market instruments include commercial papers, commercial bills, treasury bills, government securities with unexpired maturity up to one year, call or notice money, certificates of deposit, usance bills, and any other similar instruments as specified by the Reserve Bank of India from time to time.

## Classification by Investment Portfolio

Mutual fund products can be classified based on their underlying portfolio composition:

1. **First level** — based on the asset class the fund invests in, such as equity, debt, money market instruments, or gold.
2. **Second level** — based on the strategies and styles used to create the portfolio, such as Income Fund, Dynamic Bond Fund, Infrastructure Fund, Large-cap/Mid-cap/Small-cap Equity Fund, Value Fund, etc.

The portfolio composition flows from the investment objectives of the scheme.
